## The L4 finding (the substantive result of the day)

The envelope bounded **median** readout. A2.5's leakage witness is a
**joint ten-qubit measurement** whose raw survival is scaled by
∏(1−pᵢ), so a median bound is provably blind to the single outlier that
dominates the product. Applied as frozen, the formula's top pick
(`ibm_fez`) carried a **0.31 readout qubit**: a device with 97% true
sector fidelity would have presented a witness of **0.59 — below the
0.70 RED kill threshold** registered in AR-023 §17.

Cause: S2 varied readout *uniformly across qubits*, so a per-qubit
outlier was never in the model that produced the pre-commitment. This
is the C6 defect in a new place — C6 was a diagnostic that could not be
computed; this is a pre-commitment that could not see the failure it
exists to prevent.

**Owner rulings:** L4-A1 (max readout ≤ 5×10⁻²) applied; L4-A2 (readout
promoted above two-qubit error, on S2's finding that leakage binds)
ruled. Disclosed to the owner before selection: L4-A1 disqualifies bad
*paths*, not bad *backends*, so re-scanning brought `ibm_fez` back with
the best numbers overall. The owner selected **kingston anyway**, for
drift robustness — 764 qualifying paths against fez's 185.
