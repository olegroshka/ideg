## 0. Verdict

**The conjecture is true, with a two-line proof, under a spherical Muckenhoupt-type
condition.** Wherever a volume-type iff theorem could apply (weights with bounded
spherical A₂ ratio — in particular the doubling + Poincaré / A₂ class of T1), Brown's
two radial tests already decide every bounded-risk case. T1 is therefore pure
repackaging and is dead. Brown's genuinely undecided examples all have an
unbounded spherical A₂ ratio, which is exactly where Poincaré fails and the volume
test is not known to be exact. One explicit such example (a product prior with a
logarithmic one-dimensional factor) is decided here — transient, hence
inadmissible — by a product heat-kernel computation; that method is the one
Johnstone & Lalley (1984) used to answer Brown's own open question on products, so
it is prior art, and the example is an instance of their framework. Nothing new
survives except Lemma K8, which is a corollary of Brown's theorems. **Recommendation:
close Q1′ and the stream.**

## 1. Lemma K8 (spherical A₂ ⇒ Brown's radial tests are exhaustive)

Setting as in Brown (1971): X ~ N_m(θ, I), generalized prior F, marginal f* = p*F,
δ_F = x + ∇log f*. For r ≥ 1 let f̄(r) be the mean of f* over the sphere |x| = r and
A(r) the mean of 1/f* over the same sphere; put Q(r) = f̄(r)·A(r) ≥ 1 (Jensen).

**Lemma.** Suppose sup_{r≥1} Q(r) = C < ∞ ("spherical A₂"). Then:
(i) if ∫_1^∞ dr /(r^{m−1} f̄(r)) = ∞ and δ_F has bounded risk, δ_F is admissible
(Brown Thm 6.4.3 applies);
(ii) if ∫_1^∞ dr /(r^{m−1} f̄(r)) < ∞, δ_F is inadmissible (Brown Thm 6.4.4 applies),
with no risk condition.

*Proof of (ii).* For a direction φ let R(φ) = ∫_1^∞ dr /(r^{m−1} f*(rφ)). By Fubini
(all integrands positive), the mean of R over the unit sphere is
∫_1^∞ A(r) dr / r^{m−1} ≤ C ∫_1^∞ dr /(r^{m−1} f̄(r)) < ∞. Hence R(φ) < ∞ for a.e. φ,
and by Chebyshev the set Q₀ = {φ : R(φ) ≤ 2·mean R} has at least half the surface
measure and sup_{Q₀} R < ∞. This is exactly the hypothesis (6.4.7) of Brown's
Theorem 6.4.4. (i) is Theorem 6.4.3 verbatim. ∎

*Consequences.* (a) Under spherical A₂, the dichotomy "∫dr/(r^{m−1} f̄) = ∞ or < ∞"
decides admissibility of every bounded-risk δ_F, so any theorem whose hypotheses
imply spherical A₂ can add **no decidable case**. [Correction, §6: the first
version of this sentence claimed that T1's doubling + Poincaré class is such a
class "after the shell-to-sphere comparison that bounded ∇log f* provides"; the
shell-to-sphere step is fine, the ball-to-shell step was never argued. The
inclusion is proved for product weights w(x₁) by Lemma K9 and is OPEN in general.]
(b) Brown's undecided examples (his p. 900 remark) satisfy
Q(r) → ∞ along a sequence: unbounded contrast between the sphere's arithmetic and
harmonic means. (c) The lemma itself is a corollary of Brown's theorems plus Fubini;
it does not appear in Brown, Johnstone 2019, Maruyama–Strawderman 2021 or
Takanashi–McAlinn 2023 (AR-027 scope), but it is too small to carry a paper.

## 3. The undecided example, decided

Prior F(dθ) = (1+|θ₁|)^{−1} log(e+|θ₁|) dθ₁ ⊗ dθ₂ dθ₃ on R³ (flat in two coordinates):
f* depends on x₁ only, f* < ∞, K_F = R³, ∇log f* bounded (bounded risk). Brown's
tests: ∫dr/(r² f̄) < ∞ (6.4.3 silent); every ray with cos φ ≠ 0 has
∫dr/(r² f*(rφ)) = |cos φ|·∫dr/(r log r) = ∞ and the great circle has measure zero
(6.4.4 silent); Brown's single-ray variant (p. 900) silent too. So this is an
instance of his p. 900 remark.

Decision: the diffusion is the product of the 1-D diffusion W with generator
(1/w)(w u′)′ and Brownian motion on R²; Green function at 0 finite iff
∫^∞ p_t^{W}(0,0)·(4πt)^{−1} dt < ∞. With p_t^W(0,0) ≍ 4/log² t this is
(1/π)∫ dt/(t log² t) < ∞: **transient ⇒ inadmissible** (Brown Thm 5.1.1A, no
regularity). Numerically, the partial Green integral grows by
(1/(π ln 10))·1/(k(k+1)) per decade k, as predicted (0.0033 vs 0.0028 observed at
k = 6→7), total ≈ 0.126. Rigour owed: the 1-D asymptotic p_t^W(0,0) ≍ 1/m([−X_t,X_t])
for a speed measure with slowly varying growth — known territory in 1-D diffusion
theory (verify a citation before use).

**Prior art.** Brown §6.5 asked whether any non-proper-Bayes estimator is "immune to
the Stein effect" (its product with every admissible estimator of an independent
problem stays admissible). Johnstone & Lalley (1984), Z. Wahrsch. verw. Geb. 68,
29–47, DOI 10.1007/BF00535171, answered no, with Brown's recurrence
characterisation and product diffusions as the tool (Johnstone 2019, p. 663). The
example above — a recurrent 1-D generalized Bayes piece whose product with the
admissible 2-D MLE is inadmissible — is an instance of that phenomenon. The paper
itself was not accessed (Springer); whether the specific logarithmic weight appears
there is unknown and irrelevant to the verdict.

(volume doubling + scale-invariant L² Poincaré, bounded log-gradient) has

So doubling alone does not bound Q ((1+|t|)²), Poincaré alone does not bound Q

**Lemma K9 (doubling + Poincaré ⇒ A₂ on centred intervals, 1-D).** Let w > 0 be
continuous on R, μ = w dt, ν = dt/w. If μ(2I) ≤ Dμ(I) for all intervals and
∫_I|u − u_I|²dμ ≤ C|I|²∫_I|u′|²dμ for all intervals and smooth u, then
μ([−r,r])·ν([−r,r]) ≤ 32 C D³ r² for all r > 0.
*Proof.* On I = [−2r, 2r] take u = 0 on [−2r,−r], u = 1 on [r,2r] and
u(t) = ν([−r,t])/ν([−r,r]) between (energy-minimising across the middle):
∫|u′|²dμ = 1/ν([−r,r]). A function equal to 0 on mass a and 1 on mass b has
weighted variance ≥ ab/(a+b) ≥ ½min(a,b). Since [−r,r] lies in the eightfold
dilates of [r,2r] and of [−2r,−r], doubling gives min(μ([−2r,−r]), μ([r,2r]))
≥ μ([−r,r])/D³. Poincaré then reads μ([−r,r])/(2D³) ≤ C(4r)²/ν([−r,r]). ∎
Both hypotheses are necessary (table above).

**Corollary (product weights).** For m ≥ 3 and f*(x) = w(x₁) (or its Gaussian
smoothing) with w doubling + Poincaré: the sphere means are comparable to the
interval means by doubling, so Q is bounded and K8 applies; moreover reverse
doubling gives μ([−r,r]) ≥ c r^ε, hence ∫dr/(r^{m−1}f̄) < ∞ and δ_F is
**inadmissible by Brown Thm 6.4.4**. The volume criterion decides nothing new in
this class. The NO-GO verdict therefore stands for product weights, with a
proof.

**What remains open.** For non-product weights on R^m the inclusion
"doubling + Poincaré ⇒ bounded Q" is not proved; the one-dimensional mechanism
(a cheap cut through a thin set) is excluded by Lemma K9, but an escape route
that is cheap without being radial is not excluded by this argument. No
candidate was found. T1's content, if any, is confined to that corner. Not
pursued (owner's verdict stands).
