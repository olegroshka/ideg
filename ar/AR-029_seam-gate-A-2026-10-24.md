## 0. Verdict

1. **The ring weight is parabolic, not transient; δ_F is admissible, not inadmissible.** The circles of block k lie on a closed
   surface of revolution Σ_k (a graph over S²) with mesh ≤ 3.2, so above that scale Γ is a co-dimension-ONE wall. Every unit
   flow from the unit ball to infinity crosses every Σ_k and pays an energy of order 2^{k(α−2)} per block (Lemma K11, §1); the
   sum diverges for every α ≥ 2, so cap(B₁) = 0, W_F is parabolic, Brown's diffusion is recurrent, and with bounded drift
   δ_F is admissible (Brown Thm 5.1.1 B). The draft's property (i) (uniform Poincaré on all balls) fails: the two-region
   quotient on balls straddling the wall grows like ρ^{α−1} (§2, C5a), so Grigor'yan Thm 11.1 does not apply and property (iv)
   has no basis. Properties (ii) and (iii) — both Brown tests silent — are confirmed (C2, C3).
2. **Theorem 5 stands** (C6): for the cylinder weight (1+|y|²)^{α/2} on R^m, Q(r) ≍ r^{α−(m−1)} → ∞ for α > m−1 while
   doubling and the scale-invariant Poincaré inequality hold (Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio class of p-admissible power
   weights of distance to a co-dimension ≥ 2 set — citation to verify). This closes AR-028 G0 §6: in m ≥ 3, doubling +
   Poincaré do NOT force a bounded spherical A₂ ratio; Lemma K9's conclusion is one-dimensional (product weights) only.
3. **The G0 log-product example is an instance of the Johnstone–Lalley phenomenon, not of their construction.** Their
   Theorem 1.1 / Proposition 1.2 guarantee, for every null-recurrent Brownian diffusion Z^G, SOME bounded-drift recurrent
   1-D diffusion Z^H with (Z^G, Z^H) transient; their H (eqs. (3.7)–(3.11), Lemma 3.1) has a scale function growing like k at
   the scales t_k², with t_{k+1}² − t_k² ≥ e^{t_k²} (bursts of exponential growth of h). The G0 weight
   (1+|t|)^{−1} log(e+|t|) has scale s(x) ≍ x²/(2 log x) and a slowly varying speed measure in natural scale — a different
   member of the same class. Wording of record: "an explicit instance of the Johnstone–Lalley phenomenon (their Theorem 1.1),
   obtained by a different 1-D factor".
4. **Brown's undecided zone is two-sided**, and non-radial potential theory decides explicit members both ways: the G0
   product weight (inadmissible; product heat kernels / Johnstone–Lalley phenomenon) and the wall weight of this packet
   (admissible; Thomson bound along a non-spherical exhaustion). The second tool is the one AR-026 did not try: Brown's
   Theorem 4.3.1 is fully general, but his §6 evaluates it only on spheres and rays; the exhaustion-function / flux test of
   the geometric literature (Grigor'yan 1999 Thm 7.6; Thomson's principle) transfers to W_F in one line and decides a case
   his §6 cannot. AR-026's "closed negative" is narrowed accordingly: the transfer that adds a decidable case is the
   non-radial exhaustion test, not the ball-volume test.

## 1. Lemma K11 (Thomson bound along a non-spherical exhaustion) and the geometry of the ring weight

**Setting (math-note.tex Thm 6, as written).** For k ≥ 4 and j = 0, …, 2^k − 1, Γ_{k,j} is the circle {r = 2^k + j,
θ = π(j+½)/2^k}; Γ = ∪ Γ_{k,j}; f*(x) = ((1 + dist(x,Γ)²)/(1 + |x|²))^{α/2}, α ≥ 2. Then |∇log f*| ≤ α (bounded drift).

**Geometric facts (C1).** (i) The circles of block k lie on the surface of revolution
Σ_k : r = ρ_k(θ) := 2^k(1 + θ/π) − ½, θ ∈ [0, π], a closed graph over S² running from r = 2^k − ½ at the north pole to
r = 2^{k+1} − ½ at the south pole; consecutive surfaces Σ_k, Σ_{k+1} touch at the axis (south pole of one = north pole of
the next). (ii) Consecutive circles are 3.3–6.4 apart in R³ (radial step 1, meridian arc step r·π/2^k ∈ [π, 2π]); the
distance from any point of Σ_k to Γ is ≤ 3.19 (max over k ≤ 11; mean 1.2). (iii) Hence on the layer
L_k := {x : r = ρ_k(θ) + d, |d| ≤ 1} the weight satisfies 0.25·2^{−kα} ≤ f* ≤ 5.8·2^{−kα} at α = 2 (0.125–17.1 at α = 3)
(C4 columns "min/max f*·2^{kα}"). (iv) Area(Σ_k) ≍ 4^k.

**Lemma K11.** Let (R^m, δ, w dx) be a weighted Euclidean space and let {S_t}_{t∈T} be a family of pairwise disjoint
regions, each fibred by nested closed Lipschitz surfaces S_t^{(d)}, d ∈ [d₀, d₁], enclosing the unit ball, with
dV = cos(tilt) dA dd along the fibration. Then every unit flow J from B₁ to infinity has energy
∫|J|²/w dV ≥ Σ_t ∫_{d₀}^{d₁} dd / F̃_t(d), F̃_t(d) := ∫_{S_t^{(d)}} w / cos(tilt) dA. If the right-hand side is infinite,
cap(B₁) = 0 and the space is parabolic.
*Proof.* div J = 0 between B₁ and infinity, so the flux ∫ J·n dA through every S_t^{(d)} equals 1. Weighted
Cauchy–Schwarz: 1 = (∫ J·n dA)² ≤ ∫ |J·n|² cos(tilt)/w dA · ∫ w/cos(tilt) dA ≤ (∫_{S_t^{(d)}} |J|² cos(tilt)/w dA)·F̃_t(d).
Integrate in d with dV = cos(tilt) dA dd and sum over the disjoint regions. Thomson's principle (the dual of Brown's
Dirichlet principle, Thm 4.3.1): cap(B₁) = 1 / inf_J ∫|J|²/w dV (verify the continuum statement: Doyle–Snell / Soardi /
Grigor'yan 1999 §7, flux form). ∎

**Application.** Take S_k = L_k for EVEN k only (layers of blocks k and k+2 are disjoint; consecutive ones touch at the
poles), radial offsets r = ρ_k(θ) + d with |d| ≤ 1, cos(tilt) = (1 + (ρ'_k/ρ)²)^{−1/2} ∈ [0.95, 0.99]. Then
F̃_k(d) = 2π ∫_0^π f*(ρ_k(θ)+d, θ) (ρ_k(θ)+d)² + ρ'_k²) sin θ dθ ≍ C_α·2^{−kα}·4^k, so
E_k := ∫_{−1}^{1} dd/F̃_k(d) ≍ c_α 2^{k(α−2)}: constant in k for α = 2, geometric for α > 2. The sum over even k diverges,
hence cap(B₁) = 0 and W_F is parabolic. With |∇log f*| ≤ α, Brown Thm 5.1.1 (B) gives admissibility of δ_F. (Whether this
f* is a Gaussian marginal φ*F is NOT checked here — nor in the draft; the necessary condition is that e^{|x|²/2} f* be
log-convex, i.e. Hess log f* ≥ −I. To be checked in A2 for every example that is to be called "a prior".)

## 2. Why Theorem 6 (i) fails: the Poincaré quotient across the wall

On B(x₀, ρ) with x₀ ∈ Σ_k at θ = π/2 (k = 10, r₀ = 1535.5), the two-region test function u = clip((d+1)/2, 0, 1) in the
signed normal distance d to Σ_k has weighted variance ≍ μ(B) ≍ ρ³(ρ/2^k)^α and energy ≍ ρ²·2^{−kα}, so
Q_P(ρ) := Var/(ρ² Energy) ≍ ρ^{α−1}. Measured (C5a, Monte Carlo 6·10⁵ points per ball): Q_P/ρ^{α−1} → 0.041 (α = 2) and
0.0129 (α = 3) for ρ ≥ 16. A uniform scale-invariant Poincaré inequality would keep Q_P bounded. Control (C5b): the same
test-function family across a plane through the axis for Theorem 5's cylinder weight gives Q_P·ρ → 1.09, i.e. Q_P ≍ 1/ρ,
bounded — a co-dimension-two valley does not obstruct, a co-dimension-one wall does. The draft's proof of (i) argues from
balls of radius ≤ ½ (one circle each) and "John-domain chaining across the bulk" for larger balls; the chaining step is
where it fails, because the inter-ring surface Σ_k, not only the tubes, has weight ≈ 2^{−kα}.

## 4. Johnstone & Lalley (1984), read (pp. 29–45; §4 sketch of the Dirichlet-form construction skimmed)

- **Summary / Thm 1.1 (p. 30):** δ admissible and not proper Bayes in the p-variate normal problem (squared error) ⇒ there
  is an admissible δ′ in an independent univariate normal problem with (δ, δ′) inadmissible in the (p+1)-variate problem.
  "Only proper Bayes rules can be immune" (Gutmann's term). Answers Brown 1971 p. 901 negatively.
- **Prop 1.2 (p. 30):** {Z^G} null-recurrent Brownian diffusion (dZ = √2 dB + γ(Z)dt, γ = ∇log(φ*G)) ⇒ ∃ improper prior
  H on R¹ with |γ_H| ≤ C, Z^H (null) recurrent, (Z^G, Z^H) transient.
- **Prop 2.1 / Cor 2.2 (p. 32):** r dx = (φ*G) dx is the invariant measure of a recurrent Brownian diffusion; proper Bayes
  ⇔ positive recurrent. **Prop 2.3:** null recurrence ⇒ P⁰{|Z_t| ≤ a} < ε for t ≥ t₀(a, ε). **(2.5) (p. 33):** 1-D
  recurrence ⇔ ∫_0^∞ dz/(φ*G) = ∫_{−∞}^0 dz/(φ*G) = ∞ (Feller) — "underlies most of the explicit admissibility criteria in
  Sect. 6 of [Brown]". **Prop 2.4:** bounded drift ⇒ tail bound on the return time via the scale function.
- **§1 p. 31:** the Dirichlet-form reading of Brown (energy (1.6), operator (1.7) = (1/φ*G)∇·(φ*G ∇), symmetry (1.8)) —
  cites Fukushima 1980. This is the earliest explicit "Brown = Dirichlet form" statement found so far (predates Eaton 1992
  and Johnstone 1986) — update the AR-027 chain: Brown 1971 → **Johnstone–Lalley 1984 §1, §4** → Johnstone 1986 → Eaton 1992.
- **§3 Lemma 3.1 (p. 42):** H symmetric, ∫_0^{t_k²} dy/(φ*H) ≤ Ck with t_{k+1}² − t_k² ≥ e^{t_k²}, γ_H bounded ⇒ (Z^G, Z^H)
  transient. **Proof of Prop 1.2 (pp. 43–45):** h = h(0)·exp(∫₀^x β), β ∈ {0, 1} piecewise, arranged so that
  ∫_{t_k²}^{t_{k+1}²} dy/h = 1 for every k (eqs. (3.7)–(3.15)); (3.13)–(3.14): φ*H ≍ h and |(log φ*H)′| ≤ 1.
- **References worth registering:** Brown 1973, "Correction to 'Admissible estimators…'", Ann. Statist. 1, 594–596;
  Gutmann 1982, "Stein's paradox is impossible in a finite sample space", Ann. Statist. 10, 1017–1020; Gutmann 1983
  (Sankhyā A, "Decisions immune to Stein's effect"); Bhattacharya 1978, Ann. Probab. 6, 541–553 (recurrence criteria for
  multidimensional diffusions). All metadata as printed in the J–L reference list; VERIFY at the primary records.
- **Relation to the G0 example (AR-028 §3):** see §0 item 3. The explicit product (1+|θ₁|)^{−1}log(e+|θ₁|) ⊗ Lebesgue(R²)
  does not appear in J–L; it is a second explicit witness of Theorem 1.1 with p = 2, G = Lebesgue.
