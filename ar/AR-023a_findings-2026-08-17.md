## 0. One-paragraph outcome

The S1/S2 rehearsal did what a rehearsal is for: it found a defect
**before** any quota was spent — but the defect is in the pilot's own
preregistered decision rule, not in the measurement. Every physical
quantity the pilot exists to measure behaves: the endpoint reconstructs
to within 0.006 of its exact value, the sector-admissibility separation
is resolved above the reconstruction floor in **100/100** ideal and
**99/100** noisy synthetic experiments, sector leakage is GREEN, and raw
and M3-mitigated analyses agree in direction 100/100. What fails is
AR-023 §6's *success rule*: two of its five clauses are calibrated
against a floor statistic that is dimensionally mismatched to the
endpoint it bounds, and a projection budget that penalises the pipeline
for removing an estimator bias. Both failures are reported as failures;
neither is rounded into a pass. The repair is an owner ruling, proposed
in §5, and it must land **before** L4/QPU-GO.

## 2. Why the floor fails: a dimensional mismatch, measured

The frozen floor (AR-023 §6) is a **full-matrix Frobenius discrepancy**
between two independent half-shot reconstructions. The endpoint it is
compared against, ε_sector, is a **scalar** — the normalised distance
along one direction in metric space. Direct measurement of the same
reconstruction noise, decomposed (200 independent redraws):

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| reconstruction noise, full matrix norm | 1.903 |
| same noise, projected on the signal direction Δ̂ | 0.244 |
| ratio | **7.8×** |
| ratio² (effective independent directions) | **61** |
| empirical SD of the endpoint ε | 0.0152 |
| frozen floor median ÷ endpoint SD | **8.0×** |

A 10×10 symmetric zero-diagonal metric matrix has 45 free entries;
shortest-path correlations make ~61 effective directions. The endpoint
responds to **one** of them. The floor therefore overstates the
endpoint's own uncertainty by exactly the geometric factor √(effective
directions) ≈ 8 — and the measured ratio is 8.0. This is not a noisy
pipeline; it is a category error in the comparison, and it is
reproducible, quantified, and independent of shot count (both sides
scale as 1/√N, which is why escalation cannot fix it).

## 3. Why the projection clause fails: it penalises a bias correction

Clause 5 (A1.3 operationalisation) caps the projection-attributable
endpoint shift at 0.02. Measured at 768 shots:

| Estimator | Median endpoint | Bias vs exact 0.22791 |
|---|---|---|
| PSD-projected (the frozen pipeline) | 0.23381 | **+0.0059** |
| unprojected (hermitized only) | 0.32841 | **+0.1004** |

The ~0.09 "projection-attributable shift" is the projection **removing**
a 0.10 bias in the raw estimator, not introducing one. The physically
meaningful projection controls both pass with room to spare: maximum
per-RDM correction ‖ρ_proj − ρ_raw‖_F = 0.046 < 0.05 (ideal), median
0.0011 at the S2 operating point, and the projected endpoint's residual
bias is 0.0059. The clause as written would be satisfied only by a
pipeline that keeps a biased estimator.

## 5. Amendment candidates — PROPOSED, owner ruling required

These alter frozen decision rules and are therefore **not applied**.
The gate table in §1 stands as failed-as-written regardless of the
ruling.

**C1 — endpoint-level floor (recommended).** Redefine ε_floor as a
dispersion of the *endpoint*, not of the metric matrix: 2 × the
bootstrap SD of ε (equivalently the half-shot split disagreement
measured on ε itself rather than on the metric matrices). Evaluated on
the committed 768-shot battery **without re-running anything**:

| Quantity | Value | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| C1 floor, median over 100 experiments | **0.0283** | S1-G3 (< 0.05) **PASS** |
| C1 floor, q95 | 0.0329 | — |
| clause 2 (ε ≥ 2 × floor) under C1 | **100/100** | — |
| Δ = ε − floor under C1, median | **0.2054** | — |

This compares like with like and leaves both the 0.05 threshold and
the 2× multiplier untouched — only the statistic changes.

**C2 — clause 2 multiplier: no change needed.** Under C1 the multiplier
passes 100/100 (row above). If C1 is rejected, the multiplier must
instead be justified against a full-norm floor, which §2 shows is not a
like-for-like comparison.

**C3 — projection clause.** Replace "projection-attributable endpoint
shift < 0.02" with the control that carries the actual scientific
content: per-experiment maximum per-RDM ‖ρ_proj − ρ_raw‖_F < 0.05
(measured 0.0011–0.046, passes 100/100). The estimator-bias question is
already gated, at the level where it is meaningful, by **S1-G1** —
a statement about the median over experiments (0.0059, passes).
Rationale in §3: the current form rewards a biased estimator.

*Self-correction, recorded per SC-005 discipline.* A first draft of C3
also imposed the 0.02 bias tolerance **per experiment**. That is wrong
and was caught by evaluating it: a single experiment's ε has SD ≈
0.015, so |ε − ε^(37)| exceeds 0.02 in ~25% of experiments from shot
noise alone (median 0.0115, q90 0.0275). Applying a bias tolerance to
one noisy draw conflates bias with variance. The draft version scored
73/100 (768) and 80/100 (896); the corrected version scores as below.

**Counterfactual under C1 + corrected C3** (evaluated on the committed
batteries, no re-running):

| | 768 shots | 896 shots |
|---|---|---|
| success rule fires | **95/100** | **99/100** |
| S1-G2 (≥ 95) | **PASS** (at the boundary) | **PASS** |

The 768 result sitting exactly on the boundary is itself informative:
if the owner adopts C1 + C3, **896 shots is the safer operating point**
for the QPU run, and it remains inside the 450 s cap (≈ 417 s).

**S2 is not rescued by C1 + C3 at 768 shots** (conservative proxy —
see note): per-condition success would be 86/100 (fake aachen), 81/100
(fake boston), 80/100 at the mildest grid point, falling to 63/100 at
p2 = 1e-2 — all below S2-G2's 90/100. The binding sub-criterion there
is the leave-one-pair-out excursion, which §5 C5 shows is partly
structural. *Proxy note:* S2 experiment records do not store ε
bootstrap quantiles, so the C1 floor was reconstructed from the Δ
interval, which includes floor variance and therefore **overstates**
the floor — these counts are conservative lower bounds.

**C4 — record discipline.** Whatever is ruled, AR-023 §6's success rule
carries a dated amendment and the *original* rule plus this failing
battery stay in the record — the AR-020 dual-record precedent
(preregistered battery failed its own null test; reformalised battery
passed; both recorded).

**C5 — the dominance tolerance mixes structure with measurement.**
Under C1+C3 the binding failure moves to the leave-one-pair-out
excursion (20/100 at the S2 operating point). Measured on the **exact,
noiseless, infinite-shot** metric:

| Variant family | max excursion (exact) | median |
|---|---|---|
| leave-one-time-out (37) | 0.0080 | 0.0032 |
| leave-one-pair-out (45) | **0.0261** (pair (0,9)) | 0.0038 |

Removing the (0,9) edge — the two ends of the open chain — shifts ε by
0.0261 with no noise present at all: this is shortest-path graph
structure, not measurement dominance. A1.3's tolerance is 0.25 × Δ, so
it *shrinks* as noise reduces Δ: 0.0282 (S1 ideal) → 0.0206 (S2
operating point), i.e. below the structural value. The criterion
therefore penalises the exact mathematics.

Proposed form: compare like with like — require the **excess** of the
data excursion over the same variant's exact-metric excursion to be
within tolerance, rather than the raw excursion. This isolates
measurement-induced dominance, which is what clause 5 is for.

*Not evaluated:* the S1/S2 counterfactual under C5 cannot be computed
from the committed records (they store only the maximum over variants,
not per-variant values). Evaluating it requires re-running the
batteries with per-variant excursions recorded — recommended if the
owner adopts C5.
