## 1. The contribution as the paper states it

- **Theorem A (3.1).** Under doubling + scale-invariant Poincaré: parabolic ⟺ Brown's sphere test fires ⟺ the
  ball-volume test fires. So for D+P priors with bounded drift, δ_F is admissible iff Brown's 1971 sphere test says
  so; geometric criteria add decidable cases only on the inadmissible side. The ray test is not necessary for
  transience (Theorem B).
- **Theorem B (4.1).** The lattice prior on R³ (circles at dyadic scales, spacing 2^{k/2}, weight
  ((1+d²)/(1+|x|+d²))^{α/2}, α ≥ 4): D+P (Björn 2001 Prop. 6 + gluing Lemma 4.2 + one-circle Lemma 4.3 +
  Hajłasz–Koskela self-improvement), both Brown tests silent (every ray pays ≥ 0.28·r^{α/2−2} per shell;
  f̄ ≥ c), V(r) ≍ r³ ⇒ transient ⇒ δ_F inadmissible.
- **Section 5 (two-sidedness).** Prop. 5.2: circles on one surface per shell ⇒ parabolic (flux bound, Lemma 5.1),
  admissible, Poincaré fails ∝ ρ^{α−1} — the Poincaré hypothesis in the volume criterion is not decorative.
  Prop. 5.3: the log-product prior is transient (Tauberian route + numerics), an instance of Johnstone–Lalley.
- **Lemma 2.3 (comparison):** Lipschitz-log weights are, up to constants, Gaussian marginals with bounded drift.
- **Remark 2.2 (priority):** Brown's Thm 6.4.3 = the sphere-area test thirteen years before Lyons–Sullivan;
  "in the sources we have read, neither literature cites the other" (the only novelty wording used).
- **Appendix A:** Komaki's criterion as the same dichotomy on the Fisher manifold; Propositions A.1 (identity) and
  A.2 (no R); the conformal-frame remark; the open question.
